  • one-day move · #3: the date moves by one day across time zones. No swap explains it, and a parser fix does not close it.

How to tell them apart

Not by subject: all three share the same subject lines. Not by intake’s tag: it guesses from the reporter’s browser, not from the tenant. Look at the tenant, the way in, and the body.

  • parser (#1): a day-first tenant, by app, email or the booking-v2 form. The body names two dates with day and month swapped: “8 December is now 12 August”.
  • hint (#2): a booking-v3 tenant (alder.physio, copper.studio, pale.harbour), by the form. The body looks the same as parser, but the hint under the date field told the person to type the month first. The form’s hint, or how it reads the date, has to change.
  • one-day move (#3): the date moves by one day, or differs between two places: “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” The desk notes list these as oneDayMove.

For the desk

  • Before you merge into a big ticket, check that the cause matches, not just the subject. The explorer on triage shows the cause for each pair.
  • When a desk note says some reports read differently, give them a ticket of their own, not only a footnote.
